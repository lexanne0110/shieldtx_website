Compliant Confidential Trading on Public Blockchains

9 November 2026 4 min read

It's possible to trade confidentially on public blockchains like Hyperliquid while preserving complete trading records for auditors and compliance. For compliant organisations, the line between public visibility and confidential trading still requires careful analysis.

What does confidential trading mean on a public blockchain?

Confidential trading limits the information public observers can associate with a trader. Depending on the system, that might include wallet ownership, funding relationships, balances or trading activity.

The scope matters. A tool that shields the relationship between a funding wallet and a trading account does not necessarily hide the positions held by that account.

ShieldTX uses fresh trading accounts for shielded Hyperliquid trades. Orders still execute on Hyperliquid's order book. Its purpose is to separate the trader's funding wallet from their trading activity and subsequent trades.

Can trading privacy coexist with compliance?

Yes, public confidentiality and authorised oversight can coexist. An institution can seek to limit what competitors learn about its strategy while maintaining records for internal review, auditors and required disclosures.

However, a privacy feature does not replace applicable obligations. FATF's framework for virtual asset service providers includes customer due diligence, recordkeeping and suspicious transaction reporting. Which requirements apply to a particular business depends on its activities and the relevant jurisdiction's rules.

The important question is whether the confidential trading workflow supports the institution's obligations in practice.

Why do institutions need trading confidentiality?

Public wallet activity can reveal more than an individual trade. When positions are connected to a known fund or desk, observers can study its exposure, follow its trades and infer parts of its strategy.

ShieldTX's founding rationale centres on this problem: institutions want access to onchain markets without making their trading activity easy for competitors and copy bots to follow.

What should a confidential trading workflow preserve?

A useful evaluation starts with whether the institution can reconstruct its own activity.

AreaWhat the institution should verify

Frequently asked questions

Does using a privacy tool automatically make trading compliant?

No. A privacy tool addresses information exposure. Compliance requires a separate assessment of the institution, its activities, the venue and applicable rules.

Is a public blockchain history enough for an internal audit?

It may provide useful evidence, but the institution should also be able to connect accounts and transactions to its own records. Public data alone may not explain ownership, approvals or the purpose of a trade.

Do zero-knowledge proofs establish regulatory compliance?

Not by themselves. A proof establishes the specific conditions encoded in its verification logic. Proof that a transaction follows protocol rules is different from determining whether an institution meets its regulatory obligations.
