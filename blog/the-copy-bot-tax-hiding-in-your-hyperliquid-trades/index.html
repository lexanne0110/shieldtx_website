The Copy-Bot Tax Hiding In Your Hyperliquid Trades

5 November 2026 3 min read

New ShieldTX analysis finds that top perp traders being copied lose a median of 63bps per trade. And the bots copying them aren't faring much better.

Copy trading has become one of the most popular ways to participate in on-chain perps. But how much does copying actually cost, both for the copiers and for the traders being copied?

We dug into 30 days of Hyperliquid perp data to find out.

What we did

We started with every wallet that traded perps on Hyperliquid over a 30-day window: 332,317 wallets in total.

From that universe, we identified potential copiers: wallets that opened the same coin and side within 60 seconds of a leader, on at least 10 matching fills over the window.

We then measured the economic cost on both sides of the trade:

Cost to copiers. For each matched fill, we calculated the entry edge given up by arriving late. We measured how much worse the copier's entry was than the leader's, multiplied by the copier's size:

`max(copier_price − leader_price, 0) × copier_size` for buys, mirrored for sells.

We summed these values across copiers, this captures the slippage copy-bots pay by chasing the leader's price. We counted only the fills where the copier got a worse price, so this is an upper bound on what copiers pay for arriving late.

Cost to leaders. We joined copier fills to each leader burst within the 60-second window, then split bursts into those that had copier fills during execution and those that didn't. Controlling for coin, size and volatility, we compared price drift between the two groups. If copied bursts drift meaningfully more, that difference is the cost of being copied.

The headline number. For a random sample of 7 wallets on the Hyperliquid leaderboard (30-day PnL), we compared the price drift on bursts that were copied against bursts that weren't. The gap between the two is the cost of being copied. We started with 7 wallets and are expanding coverage in future reports.

What we found

Across our sample, copied bursts drifted a median of 63bps more against the leader than comparable uncopied bursts.

On a $1M position, that is roughly $6,300 lost before the trade has a chance to work. The effect was more prevalent on Altcoins.

That is the cost of being copied: the adverse drift leaders experience when copy-bots pile into their positions mid-execution. It is a hidden tax on the best traders on the platform, and it adds up on every burst that gets copied.
